# Truth and stability

**ID:** C-TRU-001  
**Status:** Open research; definitions must remain distinct  
**Origin:** Founder hypothesis that truth may be investigated through relations and that stability under change may be a relevant property.

## First distinction: expression vs proposition

A sentence can exist as a linguistic object while the proposition it expresses is false. For example, the sentence “we are happy together” can exist even if the described situation does not obtain. The existence of the sentence and the truth of its asserted content are different propositions.

## Truth conditions

A claim must be interpreted before its truth can be assessed. A useful record should specify:

- the proposition asserted;
- meanings of key terms;
- relevant context and assumptions;
- scope and boundary conditions;
- the semantics or evidence used to assess it.

A claim that appears both true and false may be ambiguous, context-dependent, approximate, or composed of several propositions. It should not be treated as a contradiction until these possibilities are examined. Alternative logics can be studied if classical logic proves inadequate for a specific purpose.

## Stability / robustness

Stability is a separate property: whether a specified feature or claim remains invariant under a specified class of transformations. A candidate schema is `S(P, T, C)`, where `P` is a proposition or property, `T` a defined set of transformations, and `C` the conditions under which validity is evaluated.

This notation is only a placeholder until a domain and invariance criterion are specified.

## Important limitation

A claim's stability does not by itself establish its truth. A false claim can remain stable because it is vague, insulated from testing, or evaluated under a restricted set of conditions. A true conditional claim can change validity when its stated conditions change.

## Open questions

- Can truth be measured directly, or do we measure evidence, confidence, robustness, or fit with specified semantics?
- What transformations should a claim survive, and which are allowed to change its truth conditions?
- Is “more true” intended to mean more probable, more robust, valid in more contexts, or something else?
- How can the framework preserve a claim as an existing expression without treating its asserted content as true?
