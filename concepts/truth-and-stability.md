# Truth and Stability

**ID:** C-TRU-001  
**Status:** Open research; no final theory asserted  
**Priority:** First foundational investigation  
**Origin:** Founder hypothesis that truth may be investigated through relations, and that stability under change may be relevant. This remains a hypothesis.

See also [Truth-First Investigation](../research/truth-first-investigation.md) and [Framework Filter](framework-filter.md).

## 1. Expression vs. proposition

A sentence or record can exist even if the proposition it expresses is false. The existence of an expression and the truth of its asserted content are distinct questions.

Before assessing truth, identify the intended proposition, its interpretation, scope, assumptions, and applicable conditions. If a sentence has multiple readings, preserve the ambiguity rather than silently selecting one.

## 2. Truth vs. assessment

Do not assume that a claim's truth is identical to our present ability to assess it. A claim can be unresolved because evidence is missing or methods are limited; this does not by itself establish that it is false.

Likewise, an assessment can be well-supported yet mistaken. The framework must preserve the distinction between what is claimed about reality and the evidence or procedure used to judge that claim.

## 3. Candidate truth conditions

A claim record should state, where applicable:

- the exact proposition asserted;
- the meanings of key terms;
- the domain, context, and assumptions;
- the scope and boundary conditions;
- the relevant reference, perspective, and time interval;
- the evidence, derivation, or assessment procedure;
- known counterevidence and limitations.

A claim that appears both true and false may be ambiguous, conditional, approximate, composed of multiple propositions, or evaluated under different conditions. Test these possibilities before concluding that a contradiction exists. Alternative logical systems may be considered if a clearly specified case warrants them.

## 4. Truth, stability, and robustness

Stability is a separate property: whether a specified feature or assessment remains invariant under a defined set of transformations and conditions.

A false claim may remain stable because it is vague, insulated from testing, or evaluated under restricted conditions. A true claim about a changing situation may cease to apply later without having been false when its original conditions held.

Therefore, stability, repeatability, and robustness may support an assessment, but none automatically establishes truth.

## 5. What does measuring truth mean?

Do not assume truth is directly measurable on a single numerical scale. Investigate whether a proposed measure is actually measuring:

- correspondence between a claim and an observed situation;
- quality, quantity, or independence of evidence;
- uncertainty or calibrated confidence;
- consistency relative to a formal system;
- repeatability or robustness under defined changes;
- predictive performance under specified conditions;
- agreement among sources or observers.

These quantities answer different questions. Any measure must define its target, procedure, scope, uncertainty, and known failure modes. Agreement, usefulness, confidence, and stability must not silently stand in for truth.

## 6. Time and conditionality

Separate at least these cases:

- the situation changes;
- the claim's stated validity interval ends;
- new evidence changes our assessment;
- a definition or interpretation changes;
- the reference frame or perspective changes.

These are not the same kind of change. Record valid time, observation time, and recording time separately when possible. Do not turn a time-indexed claim into an unconditional one by omitting its time condition.

## 7. Open questions

- What kind of object is truth attributed to?
- Is truth a property, a relation, or a more complex structure?
- Can truth be measured directly, or do procedures measure evidence, confidence, consistency, or predictive performance?
- What would distinguish an absolute claim from one whose conditions remain unstated?
- How should a framework represent competing claims, unresolved status, and partial evidence?
- What exactly can stability establish, and what can it never establish alone?
- How do truth assessment, meaning, value, and mattering interact without collapsing into one score?

No final answer is presumed. The purpose is to make the alternatives explicit and test them.
