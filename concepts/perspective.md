# Perspective

**ID:** C-PER-001  
**Status:** Working concept

## Intended meaning

A perspective is a way a case is accessed, represented, or interpreted given an observer or system, context, available information, assumptions, and representational scheme.

## Candidate schema

`I = f(E, O, C, K, A, L)`

where `E` is the event or target, `O` the observer/system, `C` context, `K` available information, `A` assumptions, and `L` the language or representation. `I` is an interpretation.

This is a proposed dependency map, not a universal psychological law. Different domains may need different variables and models.

## What can be compared?

Perspectives might be compared by:

- information available and omitted;
- assumptions and inferential rules;
- consistency with other claims;
- fit with evidence or observed outcomes;
- scope and boundary conditions;
- sensitivity to changes in context or representation.

No single criterion is guaranteed to apply to every perspective. Agreement among observers does not by itself establish truth.

## Distinctions

- Perspective-dependence is not the same as arbitrary subjectivity.
- Different interpretations may refer to different aspects of a case rather than directly contradicting one another.
- When claims conflict, check whether they use the same meanings, conditions, and scope before evaluating consistency.

## Open question

Can perspective be represented as a transformation of a shared underlying structure, and what information is invariant across those transformations?
