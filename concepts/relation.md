# Relation

**ID:** C-REL-001  
**Status:** Working concept; formal definition unresolved  
**Origin:** Founder hypothesis that connections, relations, and measurement may be different forms of a common underlying structure.

## Intended meaning

A relation expresses a connection, comparison, ordering, correspondence, dependence, or other specified association among relata.

## Candidate formalization

In mathematics, an n-ary relation over a domain `D` can be represented as a subset of the Cartesian product `D^n`. For a binary relation `R`, `R ⊆ D × D`.

This is a standard mathematical representation of a relation, not a claim that every real-world connection is already captured by a single set-theoretic model. The domain, relata, and interpretation must be specified.

## Questions

- Is every connection a relation under the intended broad definition?
- Is every relation a connection, or does “connection” imply causal, structural, or meaningful linkage?
- Can measurement be defined as a special relation, a mapping, a procedure, or a result?
- How do relations involving fictional entities work: between the represented entities within a fiction, between concepts, or between real representations and minds?
- Are relations discovered, constructed, or sometimes both?

## Examples

- `a < b`: an ordering relation on numbers.
- `distance(a, b) = d`: a quantitative relation defined by a metric on a suitable space.
- “Concept A is used to explain concept B”: a representational relation that needs an operational definition if it is to be measured.

## Caution

Do not infer that because two things can be mentioned together, a substantive relation between their referents has been established. The type and evidence for a relation matter.
